Gaps in the engine's model, found by review. Each is a decision about the model
as a whole, to be closed in the general case rather than patched where it was
noticed. None is small.

## Allowances

Each owner has one ISA and one pension allowance, shared across their accounts
of the kind. What a salary feeds, a fixed sum and the spare money all count
against it, a typed cap only lowers it, and a fixed sum or the spare money past
it is not paid, staying in the month for the accounts after it, while a
sacrifice is given up whole and what lands past it is charged as the owner's
income; what none of them takes is spent. A fixed sum that alone is past it is
refused when it is saved. It is held a twelfth a month rather than across the
tax year, so a month short of spare money loses its twelfth for good, and a sum
paid once a year is spread as if paid monthly. Each allowance is held in pounds
as the law sets it and never raised, so over a plan of decades it shrinks in
today's money as prices rise, as it would if no government changed it again.

What the law adds on top is not all held. There is no carry-forward of the three
years before, so the money purchase allowance's loss of it is moot. The taper
reads threshold income without taking off what is paid into a pension out of
taxed money, and adjusted income without a draw on a pension in the month. And
the ISA allowance is one figure, with no Lifetime ISA held to £4,000 within it.

Decide whether the allowance is held across the tax year rather than a month at
a time, which would have the projection carry it as it carries the tax year; the
rest are figures to add as the plan needs them.

## Tax

The month's income pays income tax and the employee's NI, a pension draw is
grossed up for its tax and each tax year is settled in the April after it, so
what is spare is after tax, and a pension is counted as drawn at the basic rate,
a quarter free of tax as far as its owner's lump sum allowance reaches. The draw
order is not: it is the one that was right before tax, cash, then the GIA, then
the ISA, then the pension, where filling the personal allowance from the pension
before the ISA is touched pays less over a life; with draws taxed, the engine
can now weigh the two, and does not.

Smaller gaps sit beside those. The employer's NI is credited at the full rate on
the whole sacrifice, ignoring the threshold and what the scheme passes on, and
from April 2029 a sacrifice over £2,000 a year pays both NIs. A pension paid out
of taxed money claims the basic rate and not the higher rate a taxpayer claims
back through a return; nor does its gross come off the income the personal
allowance is withdrawn against, which between £100,000 and £125,140 is worth
another 20%; and it claims relief after 75, when relief stops. The employee's NI
runs past the State Pension age, when it stops. Cash interest is not taxed, and
nor is a GIA: neither the dividends it is paid each year nor the gain a draw
realises, which would need what it cost held beside what it is worth, and an
owner for the allowances and bands it is charged under. The bands are those
outside Scotland. From April 2031 every band rises with prices together, where
the law raises the personal allowance and the basic rate band and leaves the
£100,000 the allowance is withdrawn from where it is. A draw before the pension
age is charged a flat 55% however small against the pension, and the lump sum
allowance is taken to be whole when the plan opens. The pension age is read off
the year its owner was born and not the day, so the whole of the year an age is
reached counts as reaching it, and a protected pension age some schemes give is
not held. A refund a month has no account to take goes where any money the month
leaves goes, which is nowhere the projection counts. What a pension is worth is
counted at the basic rate on the three quarters taxed, where a draw meets the
rate of the year it is taken in beside what else is earned then. A progress
point's pensions are read gross, as the sheet summed them, since a point pools
pensions without owners.

Decide the order the plan draws in, so its drawdown is after tax as its worth
is; the smaller gaps are figures to add as the plan needs them.

## Debt

A debt paid its own fixed sum is paid down to the balloon a PCP leaves, and then
paid nothing, and nothing in the plan pays the balloon: the projection holds it
where the payments left it, charged nothing, for the rest of the plan. A debt
that nothing pays is held where it opened the same way. A loan paid by a line
refinances its balloon on the same terms and runs until the whole of it clears,
so only a fixed sum leaves one standing. Charging interest on a sum nobody pays
would compound it for a lifetime, which no plan carries; in life a balloon is
paid, refinanced or settled by handing the car back, and each of those moves the
savings, the car or both.

Decide what the plan does with a balloon when the payments end, and whether a
debt nothing pays is charged its rate, so every debt the plan carries ends the
way the model says it does.

## Moves

A move sells the home and buys the next as its milestone's year opens, putting
everything the sale leaves into the purchase, borrowing the rest up to the
move's loan to value, and charging the stamp duty a home mover in England pays.
Nothing else a move costs is charged: no agent's or solicitor's fees, no survey
or removals, and no early repayment charge on a fixed rate the sale clears. What
the sale leaves over lands in the first cash account, and in a household with
none it is counted nowhere. A move is at a milestone reached at an age, a house
moves once, and a move borrows only on a loan already secured on the house, so a
home owned outright today buys the next out of the sale and the savings alone.

Decide whether a move charges its costs, as a share of each price or a sum
typed, and whether a house may move more than once, downsizing after a move up;
the rest are limits to lift as a plan needs them.
